## Questions, in order

### 1. "Introduce yourself."
**What I said:** Opened with "4.5 years at ThinkSys" straight away.
**Verdict:** 🟡 **Order was wrong.** The domains (four very different products) are the interesting part. Years come second.

### 2. "What is context engineering?"
**What I said:** Framed it as token optimisation. Long single chats degrade, so independent tasks run in agents with fresh context, repeated tasks become skills, and model choice follows the task: Haiku or Sonnet for simple work, higher models for complex work.
**Verdict:** 🟡 **Right mechanisms, wrong frame.** It sounded like cost-saving only. Lead with *quality*: what's in the window decides how well the model thinks. Cost is the second benefit. Also missing: a one-line definition before the examples.

### 3. "Which modules have you owned end to end?"
**What I said:** Only mentioned front-end work, then said I owned the graphs on CloudForestX end to end. Unclear.
**Verdict:** 🔴 **Fumbled.** No structure, and "graphs" undersold real work. You have three real answers at three depths (own products, DentScribe slice, CloudForestX dashboard layer + STS). None came out.

### 4. "A task where you disagreed with others and managed to do it your way."
**What I said:** not recorded.
**Verdict:** ⚪

### 5. "Are you at home?" (after seeing my background)
**What I said:** Yes, I've completed my notice and I'm looking. She seemed surprised I'm not working. I explained I have an offer, but it's not what I expected, so I'm looking for better.
**Verdict:** 🔴 **Defensive, and led with money.** "Not what I expected" makes the gap sound like a salary complaint. Lead with *planned decision*, then *available now*, then the offer as a calm fact.

### 6. "What is an agent?"
**What I said:** Agents run in parallel in their own context and return a summary to the main context.
**Verdict:** 🔴 **That's a subagent, not an agent.** The definition is the loop: model decides the next step, calls tools, reads results, repeats until done. Same mistake as Q2: jumped to my setup before defining the term.

### 7. "Did you write code by yourself at some point? Hands-on?"
**What I said:** "No. I've worked with AI for around 1.5 years. I don't write code myself, I review it and test as much as possible with Playwright and test cases."
**Verdict:** 🔴🔴 **The most damaging answer of the day.** The JD lists "strong programming" as a must-have. "No" reads as *can't code*. It also isn't true: you hand-wrote code for years before these tools, and you still practise without autocomplete.

- A "what is X" question twice (context engineering, agent). Both times I skipped the definition.

## Top 3 lessons
1. **Never say "I don't write code."** Say what you wrote before AI, what you still write by hand, and why.
2. **Definition first, my example second.** One sentence of textbook, then "in my setup...".
3. **List answers need a shape.** "Three, at different depths:" then one line each.

**Also:** pick one number for how long you've been AI-first. The prep pack says "about a year"; in the room you said "1.5 years". Interviewers notice when numbers move.
