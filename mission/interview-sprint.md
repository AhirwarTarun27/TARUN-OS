# Interview scheduled. Open this. Do not build a new plan.

> **The rule that governs this whole file:**
> ## Nothing enters this window that you have not already touched once.
>
> These days are a **retrieval** window, not an acquisition window. Nothing learned in a panic 72
> hours out survives contact with a real question. You are rehearsing, not studying.

---

## First: nothing changes

**Your board keeps running exactly as it is.** CV drill, coding rep, backend, applications. Untouched.

You are **not** entering interview mode. There is no interview mode. The freeze you get is caused by
the *mode switch* — building a new plan under stress, deciding where to start and when to stop and
what comes next, at your worst possible moment. That's four decisions you should never be making.

**One hour gets swapped in per day. That's the entire adjustment.**

---

## The three hours

### Day 1 — the CV, out loud

1. Read the JD once. Not four times.
2. Run `/cv-tailor` with it.
3. **Say your CV out loud, top to bottom, recorded.** Twenty minutes. Play it back once.

That's the hour. Do not open a tutorial.

> Why recorded: the gap between what you think you said and what you actually said is where interviews
> are lost. You will hear yourself trail off on exactly the bullet you were about to skip drilling.

### Day 2 — the two gaps

From the JD, pick the **two** things you can't defend. **Two.** Not the list. Not all of them.

Drill those two to *mechanism → one follow-up → boundary sentence*. Stop.

> If the JD names six things you can't defend, that is information about the JD, not a reason to study
> six things in an hour. Pick the two most likely to be asked and let the other four go. Going in with
> two solid answers beats six shaky ones.

### Day 3 — the stories

1. **Three behavioural stories**, out loud. Situation, what you did, what happened.
2. **Your boundary sentences**, out loud. Especially:
   - CloudForestX: *"I built the ingestion path and the APIs. The recommendation logic sat with the team that owned the analysis side."*
   - DentScribe: *"I built the portal, not the transcription workers, the prompts or the Sikka integration."* · *"the mobile app is a separate codebase I didn't work in."*
   - Personal projects: what **you** decided versus what the AI generated.
3. Sleep normally. This matters more than the third hour.

---

## Banned in these three days

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| New DSA problems | A pattern you meet for the first time under deadline pressure is a pattern you'll fumble on the whiteboard. |
| A new framework, library or language | Three days does not produce defensible knowledge. It produces a claim you can't survive. |
| Re-reading the whole `defend-map.md` | You'll graze all of it and own none of it. Two rows, drilled. |
| Building a new study plan | This file *is* the plan. That's the entire point of it existing. |
| Any claim you haven't said out loud before | The room is the wrong place to hear yourself say something for the first time. |

## After the interview — the part that actually compounds

**Within one hour, while it's still warm**, write into the Phase 2 gap queue in
`learning/cv-defense/progress.md`:

| Date | Company / round / JD | What it punished | Where the depth lives |
|---|---|---|---|

Then run `/console log` to record the interview.

> **This is the single highest-value thing in this file.** That table is currently empty, which means
> your syllabus is currently a guess. Every row you add replaces a guess with evidence. A wave-1
> interview that goes badly and fills three rows is a **success** — that is literally what wave 1 is
> for. You did not fail it. You bought its data.

---

## If you froze

Write down the exact question that froze you. The exact wording. Not a summary.

A freeze is a **missing prepared sentence**, not a missing skill. You almost always know the thing;
what's missing is a rehearsed way in. Write the sentence you wish you'd had, say it out loud three
times, and it's closed.

Then go back to the board tomorrow. One interview is one data point, and you pre-committed to the
sacrificial wave precisely so early losses would be cheap.
