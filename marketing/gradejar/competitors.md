# GradeJar — Competitor Acquisition Analysis

Researched 2026-07-03. Question answered: where do they get users, what's their strongest
channel, what are they NOT doing that we can do.

## The landscape (three weight classes)

### Heavyweights — generic calculator hubs
**Calculator.net, CalculatorSoup, RapidTables, Omni Calculator, GigaCalculator**

- **How they acquire:** Pure SEO at scale. Omni runs 3,700+ calculators and ~7M organic visits/mo (programmatic SEO: one template, thousands of keyword-differentiated pages). Calculator.net/RapidTables are aged domains with massive authority and rank for every "X calculator" head term. InchCalculator's model: calculators attract ~12k natural backlinks including Gizmodo/Lifehacker.
- **Strongest channel:** Google SEO + natural backlinks. Nothing else: no community, no social, no audience relationship.
- **What they miss:** Zero audience focus. Their grade pages are generic math-tool pages for nobody in particular. No teacher workflow (no gradebook, no roster, no saved state), dated UX, slow pages stuffed with ads, no voice in any teacher community, no AI-search structuring.
- **Can we out-rank them on head terms soon?** No: domain authority gap is years wide. **Do we need to?** Also no: we flank them on teacher-intent long-tails and repeat usage.

### The cult favorite — RogerHub
- **How it acquires:** 100% brand + word of mouth. 12.5M+ students served, ~100x traffic spikes in finals weeks, thousands of comment pages. Students tell students. It ranks because everyone links and searches it BY NAME.
- **Strongest channel:** Organic brand searches + student word of mouth + Reddit/forum folklore.
- **What it misses:** It's one calculator, student-only, famously unchanged for a decade. No teacher tools, no gradebook, no EZ-grader, no mobile-first polish, no content beyond the tool. The audience it ignores (teachers) is exactly ours.
- **Lesson to steal:** One tool + one audience + reliability = a moat. GradeJar's version: be the name teachers pass around ("the private gradebook that needs no logins").

### Direct rivals — niche EZ-grader/teacher sites
**gradecalculator.com, gradecalculate.com, ezgrader.us, easygradercalculator.com, gpacalculator.net, teacherhelp.org, grade-assistant.com, essaygrader.ai's free tool page**

- **How they acquire:** Small-site SEO on "ez grader"/"easy grader" terms. Some (essaygrader.ai) use the free calculator as top-of-funnel for a paid AI product. teacherhelp.org rides a content blog.
- **Strongest channel:** Long-tail Google SEO. A few dabble in content; none show real community presence, Pinterest strategy, or PR footprint.
- **Weaknesses across the board:** thin single-tool sites, no privacy story, no gradebook (the retention feature), mediocre CWV, no structured data depth, no AI-search readiness, no brand. GradeJar already benchmarked #1 against this class on build quality.
- **These are the ones we beat first.** They hold rankings by default, not by strength.

## Content gaps nobody fills (our openings)

1. **Teacher-first everything.** Every competitor is student-facing or generic. "Grade a stack of tests fast", roster workflows, department-sharing: unowned territory.
2. **The privacy/FERPA story.** No competitor says "student grades never leave your browser." Teachers care, districts care, edtech press cares. This is the PR wedge AND a differentiator AI assistants can repeat.
3. **AI-search structuring.** None of the niche rivals structure for citations. First mover in the niche wins the "what's a good free gradebook" answer in ChatGPT/Perplexity.
4. **Pinterest/community presence.** The heavyweights don't do audiences; the niche sites don't do marketing. A consistent Pinterest + Reddit presence is uncontested.
5. **The N-questions long tail.** "ez grader 25 questions"-style searches are served by clunky chart pages. Programmatic, fast, clean pages win these quietly.
6. **Recurring use.** Everyone else is a calculator (visit, compute, leave). The gradebook makes GradeJar a weekly habit: more sessions per user than any rival can get, which is exactly what AdSense pays for.

## Quick wins (ordered)

1. Get indexed + into GSC/AdSense (we currently lose to everyone by not existing in Google).
2. Unblock AI-search crawlers (rivals haven't thought about this; cheap first-mover win).
3. Pitch back-to-school tool roundups in July (rivals never pitch anything).
4. Ship `/ez-grader/N-questions` programmatic set (direct strike at the niche rivals' bread and butter).
5. Seed the privacy story on Reddit + SOS/Qwoted (no competitor has a story at all).
