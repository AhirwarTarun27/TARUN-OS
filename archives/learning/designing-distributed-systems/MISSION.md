# Mission: Designing Distributed Systems (Burns, 2nd ed.)

## Why
Get fluent enough with distributed-system patterns to reason out loud in system
design interviews — name the right pattern, defend the tradeoff, and not freeze
on the "how would you scale this?" follow-up. This quarter's interview-prep goal,
grounded in a real book instead of scattered blog posts.

## Success looks like
- In an interview, I can name a pattern (sidecar, sharding, scatter/gather, work queue…) and say *when* and *why* to reach for it, plus its main tradeoff.
- I can whiteboard a scalable, reliable service and defend the design choices.
- I have a compressed mental map of the 16 chapters I can review the night before an interview.

## Constraints
- Concepts-first. Minimal setup — no standing up real Kubernetes clusters unless a pattern truly needs it.
- Short sessions. Working-dev time budget, learning around building GradeJar + JsonBeam.
- Learn chapter by chapter, in book order, but framed through the interview lens.

## Out of scope
- Becoming a Kubernetes operator / cluster admin.
- Deep hands-on YAML labs (for now — may revisit for a couple of marquee patterns).
- Classic algorithms/DSA (that's a separate track).
